# Polygood house style for email

Adapted from `polygood-case-tracker/docs/case-study-outline.md` (the outline for case pages on polygood.com), with additions for email. When the two disagree on something that only concerns the website, the case-study outline wins for the website and this file wins for email.

## Names

- **Polygood®** on first mention in the email, **Polygood** after. The company is **The Good Plastic Company**.
- **Colour (pattern) names** as polygood.com spells them on product pages, in title case: Salmon Terra, Emerald Ghost, Sapphire Terrazzo, White Terrazzo, Black Lollipop, Translucent Glitter Gold, Maldives, Terrazzo Nuovo, Timeless Duo, Salt Dune, Fleur de Sel. The Notion catalogue sometimes misspells them ("White Terazzo", "Black lolipop", "Emerald ghost"); correct these in the email.
- **Product links** follow `https://polygood.com/product/<colour-slug>/`, lower case with hyphens (`salmon-terra`, `emerald-ghost`). Check the page exists before relying on a guessed slug; if unsure, mark `[check URL]`.
- **Sample links** go to the "Order Samples" page linked in the polygood.com footer. Check its current URL before the first send and reuse it; in 2026 search results still showed the older shop at thegoodplasticcompany.com/product-category/samples/, so confirm which one is live.
- **Project links** follow `https://polygood.com/projects/<short-name>/`. Use the catalogue's `siteUrl` when it has one. A project without a case page can still appear, but link it to the closest real page and say so in the notes.
- **Client, venue and partner names** are spelled the way they spell them, accents included: Module Carré, Les Rendez-vous de la Matière, Wallpaper*.

## Case and punctuation

- Sentence case for subject lines, headings and buttons: "Order a sample", not "Order A Sample".
- No exclamation marks, in the subject line or the body.
- One spelling standard: **UK English** (colour, programme, centre), matching the URLs already on the site.
- Spec-style lines (Colours used, Location, Credits) are `Label: value`, no full stop.

## Words

- Every paragraph carries at least one fact: what was made, from what, where, by whom, how much.
- Cut "unique", "inspiring", "stunning", "game-changing", and lines like "gained new meaning and impact". Say what the material is instead ("remnants of refrigerators, TV parts and keyboards").
- No invented numbers, quotes or claims. Recycled content, tonnes saved, certifications and customer quotes come from the user, the catalogue or the case page, or the email gets a `[fact needed]` placeholder.
- Link the client and the partner once each, on their names.

## Lengths

| Element | Length |
|---|---|
| Subject line | about 50 characters or fewer |
| Preview text | 40–90 characters |
| Project title | client or venue, plus ", City" when helpful; under 30 characters |
| One-line description | 10 words or fewer |
| Hero story | 40–70 words |
| Other stories | 30–50 words |
| Whole email | readable in about two minutes; 8–12 tracked links |

## Credits and rights

- Credit photographers, designers and fabricators in the wording and order the partner asks for. The catalogue's `credits` field records requests such as "SAMJI on the first credit line, with a link".
- Don't use a project whose catalogue status is `nda` or `restricted`, or whose `rights` note says the licence expired or forbids promotional use. When in doubt, ask.

## Example block

```text
De Bijenkorf
Floor, counters and stands for the Dutch department store

[Photo: widest shot of the space first.
 Alt text: "Counter in [colour] at De Bijenkorf, [city]"]

[fact needed: what De Bijenkorf asked for]. Random Studio designed the pieces
and Fiction Factory built them from Polygood® panels.
[fact needed: one result, such as the number of pieces or stores]

Colours used: [colour 1] · [colour 2]
→ See the project  [polygood.com/projects/de-bijenkorf/ · utm_content=hero]
→ Order a sample of [colour 1]  [sample page · utm_content=sample-hero]

Photography: Milenka Backx · Design: Random Studio · Fabrication: Fiction Factory
```

Everything stated in this example comes from the catalogue entry (what was made, the country, the design, fabrication and photo credits). The brief, the result, the city and the colours aren't in it, so they stay as placeholders for the user to fill in, rather than being made up.
