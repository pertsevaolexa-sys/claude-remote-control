# Brand, claims and voice

Read this before writing or editing any words in the email: subject lines, the intro, chapter text, credits and the open call.

## The premium rule

The user's words: "it is very important that we do not make the connotation about the fact that litter used to be our panels, we are premium brand".

Polygood® is sold as a premium surface material to designers, architects and makers. So the email never names or hints at what the plastic was before. Leave out:

- fridges, freezers, CD cases, cable spools, cutlery, appliances, electronics, packaging, bottles;
- "old", "waste", "litter", "rubbish", "used to be", "first life", "second life", "made from", "turned into", "rescued", "diverted from landfill".

Talk instead about colour, pattern, finish, how the material is worked, the designers and makers, and the spaces. Pattern captions give the code (`#PS1706`) because that is what a specifier needs to order a sample.

"Recycled" and "recyclable" may appear when a claim needs them (the take-back programme), but never as the opening idea of a chapter. Lead with design.

## Claims you can make

These were checked on polygood.com (tech guide, stain test, cleaning guide, take-back page, product pages) in November 2026. Wording that was approved in a sent edition is given as written. Before reusing a claim, open the page again: polygood.com changes.

| Claim | Wording used | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Solid all the way through | "Polygood is solid all the way through, so the front edge of a worktop needs no edging strip and can become part of the design." | The edge can be shaped (Maison Kyka's wave edge) |
| Thermoforming | "heated an Emerald Ghost panel until it softened, bent it into a curve" | Name the maker who did it |
| Workable with standard tools | "a material your workshop can cut, shape and finish with standard tools"; "can be cut, sanded and polished like a solid surface" | |
| Stain test | "In our stain test with 20 everyday agents, most left no trace on Polygood, and a mark that stays can be sanded out." | Don't round "most" up to "all" |
| Daily care | "Daily care is a pH-neutral cleaner, with a trivet for hot pans." | Never say heat-proof |
| Moisture | "It absorbs no moisture, so the sink can sit directly under the countertop." | Not "waterproof" |
| Food contact | "tested for food contact" | Not "certified food safe": the evidence is a test report |
| Warranty | "a 10-year limited warranty for interior use" | Keep "limited" and "interior" |
| Support for makers | free sample boxes for industry professionals, advice on your project, the technical guide and adhesive matrix, custom pattern development, the take-back programme | For the open call or partner box |

## Do not claim

- **Cradle to Cradle certified.** The certificate shown on polygood.com expired on 11 June 2026. Check for a renewal before mentioning it. A client's own concept "inspired by Cradle to Cradle" can be reported as theirs.
- **CO₂ figures.** The numbers differ between polygood.com pages.
- **Superlatives and absolutes**: indestructible, scratch-proof, heat-proof, waterproof, maintenance-free, "the most sustainable".
- **Anything in [square brackets] in Notion.** Brackets mark facts still to confirm with the partner. Leave them out and list them in the edition README.
- **Colour descriptions from drafts.** Check them against the swatch. The Notion draft called Timeless Duo Blue "deep blue", but it is white with blue flecks.
- **Making details nobody confirmed**, for example "invisible glued joints", which part of a kitchen is thermoformed, or a project's town or brief.
- **Partnership terms.** No discounts, exclusivity or programmes that haven't been agreed.
- **"Firsts" stated as fact.** Attribute them: "They call it Austria's first Circular Living hotel".

## Voice and style

- Use UK spelling: colour, programme, centre, specialise, metre.
- No exclamation marks.
- No em or en dashes as punctuation. Use a colon, a comma or a new sentence. Hyphens in compounds ("take-back", "10-year") are fine.
- Don't put small labels in capitals. The user said capitals labels like "SHAPE THE CURVE" look AI-made. Use sentence case in italics: "Shaped in the workshop", "Open call to kitchen makers".
- Avoid slogan headings and AI tics: "Shape the curve", "Where design meets…", "Not just X, but Y", "elevate", "seamless", "game-changer", "in the heart of", triplets for rhythm, rhetorical questions.
- Use plain framing. The user found "fitted, waved and left it bare" strange; say what the designer did and why it works ("an interesting design decision"), not a riddle.
- Give each chapter one job. Each project shows one thing Polygood does in that kind of space: the curve, the edge, stains and care, daily use at work. Write it as the problem, then what the material did, then the result for the people using it. Aim for 3 to 5 sentences, 50 to 75 words.
- Lead with the people. Name the designer, the maker and the client the way they name themselves.
- Leave out what doesn't serve the reader:
  - "When a kitchen is finished we share it" was cut as unimportant.
  - The take-back programme was cut from an office-pantry chapter where it didn't fit.
  - "Even in white" was cut as condescending about a pattern.
- Write "Polygood®" with the ® in the intro and headings, and "Polygood" in running text.
- Put numbers in figures: "20 everyday agents", "10-year".

## Credits

Every project gets a credits line for design, fabrication or installation, client and photography, in this form:

`Fabrication and design: Samji Studio · Design: Kyka · Client: Maison Kyka · Photography: Samji Studio`

Partners have their own rules. Keep to them:

| Partner | Rule |
|---|---|
| Samji Studio (Paris) | Samji on the first credit line, linked to https://www.ateliersamji.com/, then manufacturer, client and photographer as listed on Samji's site |
| CBRE Design Collective | Credited together with the client: "Design: CBRE Design Collective · Client: Commerzbank AG" |
| All Art Design | Credited together with jesperproductions.nl |
| WUNSCH (Lewes, Sussex) | "Cabinetmaking and photography: WUNSCH", linked to https://www.wunsch.co.uk/ |
| Henriette Stadthotel | Photographer Patrick Johannsen Fotografie (still to confirm against supersusi.com) |

If a credit is unknown, leave that role out of the email and add "confirm the photographer" to the README checklist. Never guess a name.

Show partner logos only once the partner has agreed. Until then, put "confirm logo use with …" in the checklist.

## Calls to action

- **One action per pattern.** Each pattern gets an "Order sample" link to its product page, `https://polygood.com/product/<pattern-slug>/`. Each project gets one filled "See the project" button to `https://polygood.com/projects/<slug>/`.
- **The open call or partner box goes last.** It says what working with Polygood gives a maker, not what Polygood gets. Its button goes to https://polygood.com/contact-us/ unless the user gives an email address.
- **Past campaigns.** This is from 44 campaigns, October 2024 to September 2026: average opens 23.0%, clicks 2.8%.
  - Single, concrete actions got the most clicks. "Order samples" did best; "Contact us" did worst.
  - Subjects with the brand and the season opened best.
  - Thursday at 13:00 UTC had the best click rate of the busy slots.

## Subject lines and preview text

Write three subject options and one preview line. Put them in copy.json (`subject_lines`, `preview_text`) and in the chat. The user enters them in Mailchimp settings.

- Option 1: the brand plus the edition's idea, for example "The life of a Polygood® worktop, in four kitchens".
- Option 2: concrete, naming the places or patterns.
- Option 3: the open call or a curiosity angle.

No "!" here either, and no ALL CAPS words. The preview text adds what the subject doesn't say.
