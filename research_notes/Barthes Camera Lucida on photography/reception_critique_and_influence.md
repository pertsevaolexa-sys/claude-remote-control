# Reception, Critique and Influence of Roland Barthes's Camera Lucida (La Chambre claire, 1980), 1980–2026

Method note for the report writer: page fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by the session's egress proxy for every domain tried (mitpress.mit.edu, en.wikipedia.org, eprints.soton.ac.uk, librarysearch.aut.ac.nz). All findings below come from web-search results: catalogue records, abstracts, publisher blurbs and review excerpts. No full articles were read. Where a detail comes from my own background knowledge and no retrieved source confirmed it, it is in the Gaps sections and marked "unverified". Barthes quotations are from Richard Howard's English translation (Hill and Wang / Farrar, Straus and Giroux, 1981) unless stated. ## 1. Major responses and their arguments (Derrida, Sontag, Burgin, Krauss, Fried, Elkins, Olin, Batchen, Silverman, Prosser, Knight)

### Takeaway
The responses come in waves. First came mourning tributes in 1980–81 (Derrida, Sontag). Then psychoanalytic and semiotic rereadings in the 1980s–90s (Burgin, Knight). Then a 2005 *Critical Inquiry* quarrel over the punctum (Fried vs. Elkins). Then empirical "it's not what he says" critiques (Olin), and Batchen's 2009 MIT Press anthology *Photography Degree Zero*, which made the book a field of scholarship in its own right. Later work (Silverman 2015; Elkins 2011) defines itself *against* the book's indexical and mournful model while still conceding its centrality.

### Cited Findings

**Jacques Derrida**
- Derrida's essay was first published as "Les morts de Roland Barthes," *Poétique*, no. 47 (1981). It is described as an obituary and farewell that is also a tribute, written shortly after Barthes's death; *La Chambre claire* itself had come out only weeks before he died. — [nbpublish.com article](https://en.nbpublish.com/library_read_article.php?id=71369)
- Derrida reads the book as a text about ghosts that "permeate its fabric and logic and form the category of 'punctum'." — [nbpublish.com](https://en.nbpublish.com/library_read_article.php?id=71369)
- For Derrida, writing after Barthes's death, *Camera Lucida* "becomes its own kind of wound, an occasion for rereading and for mourning." He uses Barthes's plural "deaths" to trace links between death, language and thought. — [UT Austin Viz: Roland Barthes' Camera](https://viz.dwrl.utexas.edu/old/content/roland-barthes-photography.html)
- The essay is collected in Derrida, *The Work of Mourning*, ed. Pascale-Anne Brault and Michael Naas (University of Chicago Press, 2001). The book gathers Derrida's texts on the deaths of Barthes, de Man, Foucault and others. — [NYU Medical Humanities entry](https://medhum.med.nyu.edu/view/12930); [biblio.com listing](https://www.biblio.com/9780226502496)
- Derrida's reading is still being extended: e.g. "Barthes avec Derrida, ou vers un après-punctum social et écologique" in *Revista Transilvania*. — [Revista Transilvania](https://revistatransilvania.ro/barthes-avec-derrida-ou-vers-un-apres-punctum-social-et-ecologique/)

**Michael Fried**
- "Barthes's Punctum," *Critical Inquiry* 31, no. 3 (Spring 2005): 539–574. — [Critical Inquiry, Spring 2005 issue](https://criticalinquiry.uchicago.edu/past_issues/issue/spring_2005_v31_n3); [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/FRIBP)

- A later critique of Fried's reading: "The Theatricality of the Punctum: Re-Viewing Roland Barthes' Camera Lucida," *Performance Philosophy*. — [Performance Philosophy](https://www.performancephilosophy.org/journal/article/view/126)

**James Elkins**

**Margaret Olin**
- Olin's *Touching Photographs* (University of Chicago Press, 2012) has a chapter titled "Roland Barthes's 'mistaken' identification." — [CSSSC catalogue TOC](https://catalog.cssscal.org/Record/csssc.33736/TOC); [CCA record](https://www.cca.qc.ca/fr/recherche/details/library/publication/711050918)

### Inferences
- Fried and Elkins (2005) mark the point where the book stopped being read mainly as an elegy or semiotic text and became a contested resource for art-world arguments about photography as art. Fried's appropriation helped revive the book in the 2005–2010 period.
- The two "readings through the lens of race" in Batchen's volume are probably those by Carol Mavor and Shawn Michelle Smith. The publisher's "two responses" to Fried are Elkins's and probably one other contributor's (perhaps Krauss's). Neither is confirmed.
- One pattern runs across the critics: the book's authority rests on a photograph no reader can see, so each critic either supplies a theory for the absence (Derrida's spectrality, Prosser's Real) or tests Barthes's descriptions against the visible evidence (Olin).

## 2. The Winter Garden Photograph debates: does it exist, and is it what Barthes says?

### Takeaway
Barthes describes the photograph of his mother aged five but refuses to reproduce it. That refusal has produced three positions. Some read the absence as the book's structural or rhetorical core. Olin argues the photo as described may never have existed and is a reworking of another family photograph, so the book reads more like a novel. Artists treat the unseen image as an invitation, as in Odette England's 2020 *Keeper of the Hearth*.

### Cited Findings
- Barthes (Howard trans., 1981, p. 73; section 30, unverified): "I cannot reproduce the Winter Garden Photograph. It exists only for me." He adds that for the reader it would be "nothing but an indifferent picture". The remark is parenthetical. — [UT Austin Viz](https://viz.dwrl.utexas.edu/old/content/roland-barthes-photography.html); [Macaulay CUNY seminar notes](https://eportfolios.macaulay.cuny.edu/seminars/bergman09/content/camera-lucida-part-ii/index.html)
- The snapshot is dated 1898 and shows Henriette Barthes aged five. It is the only image Barthes discusses at length without reproducing it. — [Photoworks, "The Great Unknown"](https://photoworks.org.uk/great-unknown/); [UT Austin Viz](https://viz.dwrl.utexas.edu/old/content/roland-barthes-photography.html)
- Margaret Olin has suggested the book should be read as a novel and that the photograph may never have existed. — [Photoworks](https://photoworks.org.uk/great-unknown/)
- Olin's specific claim: the Winter Garden image as described is an imagined alteration of another picture, with the grandfather removed and a winter garden added. — [IVC review of *Touching Photographs*](https://ivc.lib.rochester.edu/?p=4802)
- Commentators call the withholding "a brilliant rhetorical manoeuvre" that invites each reader to project their own lost loved one into the void. Others argue the photograph must be an "absent presence" to sustain the book's deconstructive dynamic. — [Photoworks](https://photoworks.org.uk/great-unknown/); [Kansas State STTCL article](https://ksu.janeway.systems/sttcl/article/11900/galley/11784/download)
- The missing photograph has a "doppelgänger": the colour Polaroid by Daniel Boudinet that Barthes reproduces at the start of the book but never discusses. — [Photoworks](https://photoworks.org.uk/great-unknown/)
- Like Proust, Barthes describes a photograph he does not reproduce, and he compares the experience to Proustian involuntary memory. — [Kansas State STTCL](https://ksu.janeway.systems/sttcl/article/11900/galley/11784/download)
- Odette England, *Keeper of the Hearth: Picturing Roland Barthes' Unseen Photograph* (2020): more than 200 photographers, writers, critics, curators and historians made visual and textual responses to how they imagined the photograph. It was published as a 320-page volume with a five-month exhibition at the Houston Center for Photography, tied to the book's 40th anniversary. — [LensCulture](https://www.lensculture.com/articles/odette-england-keeper-of-the-hearth); [F-Stop Magazine review](https://fstopmagazine.com/blog/2020/book-review-keeper-of-the-hearth-picturing-roland-barthes-unseen-photograph-by-odette-england); [CPH Mag](https://cphmag.com/barthes-unseen/); [Don't Take Pictures](https://www.donttakepictures.com/dtp-blog/2020/10/17/weekend-reading-keeper-of-the-hearth); [LFI](https://lfi-online.de/en/stories/odette-england-keeper-of-the-hearth-14950.html)
- Marjorie Perloff's essay on Christian Boltanski pairs Barthes's Winter Garden photograph with Boltanski's archival photo-installations. — [Perloff, EPC/UPenn](https://writing.upenn.edu/epc/authors/perloff/boltanski.html)

### Inferences
- The scholarly "non-existence" thesis rests mainly on Olin. Most other "it doesn't exist" claims in search results come from student essays and blogs and should not be treated as scholarship.
- The debate has moved from fact (does the print survive?) to genre (is *Camera Lucida* theory, memoir or fiction?). That makes it relevant to the "text and concepts" researcher.

- The title of the Perloff essay was not confirmed in retrieved text. Background (unverified): "'What Has Occurred Only Once': Barthes's Winter Garden/Boltanski's Archives of the Dead."

## 3. The indexicality debate: digital, smartphone and AI images vs. "that-has-been"

### Takeaway
Barthes's "noeme" of photography, "That-has-been", became the reference point for the "index" in theory. Since the 1990s digital turn it has been challenged in three ways. Some argue digital manipulation ends photography's assumed veracity (Mitchell 1992). Some argue the index was always a weaker concept than claimed (Snyder, Gunning, Doane 2007). Since c. 2022, some argue generative AI swaps "that-has-been" for "that-could-be" (RIFL 2026; Fontcuberta 2025). Defenders and revisers include Silverman (analogy rather than index) and Sebald scholarship, which shows writers resisting the index.

### Cited Findings
- Barthes's formulation (Howard trans.; section 32, unverified): "The name of Photography's noeme will therefore be: 'That-has-been'". The photograph is described as never separated from its referent. — [U Chicago CSMT annotation](https://csmt.uchicago.edu/annotations/barthescamera.htm); [Supersummary plot summary](https://www.supersummary.com/camera-lucida/summary/)
- William J. Mitchell, *The Reconfigured Eye: Visual Truth in the Post-Photographic Era* (MIT Press, 1992), is credited as an early full treatment of digital imaging's ethical and legal stakes and the loss of the photograph's assumed veracity. It is central to debates on digital photography. — [AbeBooks listing](https://www.abebooks.com/9780262132862/Reconfigured-Eye-Visual-Truth-Post-Photographic-0262132869/plp); [OAPEN study](https://library.oapen.org/bitstream/20.500.12657/37408/1/9783839439029.pdf)
- Mary Ann Doane (ed.), "Indexicality: Trace and Sign," special issue of *differences* (2007), including Tom Gunning, "Moving Away from the Index: Cinema and the Impression of Reality". Gunning's earlier "What's the Point of an Index? or, Faking Photographs" dates from 2004. — [Duke University Press](https://dukeupress.edu/indexicality-trace-and-sign)
- In the "Art Seminar" debate (Elkins's *Photography Theory* volume), Joel Snyder is reported as arguing that index claims confuse photons with objects. — [Wasielewski, Aalto thesis PDF, 2026](https://helsinkiphotomedia.aalto.fi/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/WASIELEWSKI_AMANDA_Light_in_the_Distance.pdf)
- A Korean study argues that the claim that digital imaging ended indexicality relies on a reading of the index different from Barthes's, Bazin's, Benjamin's and Peirce's. — [Sookmyung ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.sookmyung.ac.kr/item/88775173-b98f-45e0-a515-dd4bf2cad23c)
- A 2026 article in *RIFL* argues that for AI photorealistic images "that-has-been" gives way to "that-could-be". Drawing on Eco, it says photography's "certification of presence" is replaced by a "certification of plausibility". — [RIFL](https://rifl.unical.it/index.php/rifl/article/view/944)
- Joan Fontcuberta, *Against Barthes: The Eye and the Index* (MACK, DISCOURSE series, April 2025; 208 pp.; ISBN 978-1-915743-93-0; £14). It "refutes, as well as draws on" Barthes's claim that every photograph says "this has been". It asks whether the camera records reality or performance and says post-photography and generative AI complicate the question. Visual essays from the Mexican tabloid *Alerta* (1960s–80s) are interleaved. — [MACK](https://mackbooks.co.uk/products/against-barthes-the-eye-and-the-index-joan-fontcuberta); [Moom](https://moom.com.tw/en/item/against-barthes-the-eye-and-the-index)
- Fontcuberta's earlier exhibition and book *Ça-a-été? Contra Barthes* ran at La Virreina Centre de la Imatge, Barcelona. Sources disagree on dates: 15 Oct 2021–16 Jan 2022 per the city guide, and 10 Oct 2021–13 Feb 2022 per a catalogue record; a February 2022 event page still lists the show as on. — [Barcelona Cultura](https://www.barcelona.cat/barcelonacultura/en/recomanem/new-exhibitions-virreina-october-2021); [La Virreina publication page](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/lavirreina/ca/publicacions/ca-ete-contra-barthes/568); [La Virreina book presentation](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/lavirreina/en/ca-a-ete-contra-barthes-joan-fontcuberta); [La Virreina exhibition page](https://ajuntament.barcelona.cat/lavirreina/en/exhibitions/ca-ete-against-barthes/542)
- Kaja Silverman's "analogy" model is explicitly set against the indexical model associated with Barthes. — [nonsite.org](https://nonsite.org/the-miracle-of-analogy)
- Popular commentary: a Substack essay argues that, on Barthes's logic, AI images cannot have a punctum because they are averages of training data. (Blog, not scholarship.) — [Ellis and Eyre Substack](https://ellisandeyre.substack.com/p/what-every-ai-image-is-missing)

### Inferences
- Debates about photographic truth after smartphones and AI keep returning to Barthes because "that-has-been" is the clearest, most quotable statement of the index thesis. Fontcuberta's two "against Barthes" projects (2021–22 and 2025) show the book serving as the standard opponent.
- Defenders and critics split on whether "that-has-been" is an ontological claim about chemistry and light, which digital and AI images would undercut, or a phenomenological claim about how a viewer believes, which survives as long as viewers trust an image (cf. Šimić 2025, Section 1).

### Gaps
- I did not find a specific scholarly treatment of the *smartphone* image (as opposed to "digital" or "AI") in relation to Barthes.
- Authors of the RIFL 2026 article were not retrieved.
- Fred Ritchin, *After Photography* (2009), Laura Mulvey, *Death 24x a Second* (2006), and Geoffrey Batchen's digital-era writings are likely relevant but were not searched.

## 4. Influence on artists, writers, filmmakers and photography teaching; standing vs. Sontag's On Photography and Benjamin's Little History

### Takeaway
*Camera Lucida* is a fixture of photography and art-history syllabi alongside Sontag's *On Photography* (1977) and Benjamin's "Little/Small History of Photography" (1931). The MIT Press blurb calls it "perhaps the most influential book ever published on photography". Its strongest literary afterlife is in photo-text prose: W. G. Sebald (complicated and partly oppositional), Teju Cole (*Blind Spot*, NYT column), and artist projects built on the unseen Winter Garden photograph.

### Cited Findings
**Writers**
- Kevin Brazil, "W. G. Sebald's revisions of Roland Barthes," *Textual Practice* 33, no. 4 (2019): 567–584, DOI 10.1080/0950236X.2017.1308961. James Wood speculated that *Austerlitz* is "in deep dialogue" with *Camera Lucida*. Archival work shows that Sebald first read Barthes in the early 1990s. His practice in *The Emigrants*, which erased an image's indexical link to its referent, rejects the book's central ontological claim. He returned to Barthes in the late 1990s while writing *Austerlitz* and revised the draft so the published novel refers directly to his reading of *Camera Lucida*. — [Southampton ePrints record](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/397722/)
- Sebald's *Vertigo* has been read in relation to the punctum, which Barthes says works metonymically. — [Western Sydney University](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/photography-memory-metonymy-or-wg-sebalds-vertigo/)
- Neil Badmington, *Punctum Saliens: Barthes, Mourning, Film, Photography*, is an essay on the book's ties to mourning and film. — [PhilPapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/BADPSB); [Cardiff ORCA](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/35996/)
- Teju Cole wrote the "On Photography" column in *The New York Times Magazine*, starting with "A True Picture of Black Skin" (February 2015). A *Ploughshares* review says *Blind Spot* (2017) "owes more to Barthes, whose Camera Lucida theorized good photography as that which wounds." Another critic says that in Cole the studium is passed over and the affective punctum response is what matters. — [Ploughshares blog](https://pshares.org/blog/the-role-of-images-in-teju-coles-blind-spot/); [Steven Kasher press PDF](https://stevenkasher.com/attachment/en/5491696ae253608b417b23c6/Press/5952859df6c038b95c73852e)

**Teaching and canonical standing**
- Wikipedia says the book, with Sontag's *On Photography*, was one of the most important early academic books of criticism and theory on photography. — [Wikipedia: Camera Lucida (book)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camera_Lucida_(book))
- The University of Edinburgh history-of-photography course reads Benjamin's 1931 "Small History of Photography", Sontag's *On Photography* (1977) and *Camera Lucida* (1980) as the central twentieth-century texts on photographs' social, cultural and psychological roles and their indexical nature. — [Edinburgh DRPS course](https://www.drps.ed.ac.uk/13-14/dpt/cxhiar11047.htm)
- John Cabot University assigns *Camera Lucida* and *On Photography* as required reading. Universidade Lusófona surveys Barthes, Sontag, Benjamin and Flusser. — [John Cabot syllabus](https://myjcu.johncabot.edu/syllabus/syllabus_print.aspx?IDS=10022); [Lusófona](https://www.ulusofona.pt/en/lisboa/bachelor/photography/ULHT624-17592)
- Martha Schwendener (*Flusser Studies* 18) notes that this core theory canon is Euro-American. Benjamin's essays reached a wide English readership only in the late 1960s–70s, and she places *Camera Lucida* within a late-1970s theoretical turn. — [Flusser Studies PDF](https://www.flusserstudies.net/sites/www.flusserstudies.net/files/media/attachments/schwendener-vilem-flusser-theories-photography.pdf)
- Elkins concedes the book "remains a central text" and that the punctum is a key concept of photography theory, even while writing against it. — [Elkins PDF](https://jameselkins.com/wp-content/uploads/2009/05/what-photography-is.pdf)
- *Mosaic*'s 2020 anniversary introduction says responses range from dismissal to treating the book as a recurring theme of photography theory. — [Mosaic 53.4 intro PDF](https://mosaic.umanitoba.ca/assets/pdf/issue_intro/intro_53_4.pdf)

### Inferences
- In standing, the three texts form a teaching triad. Benjamin is read for history and technology, Sontag for ethics and the consumption of images, and Barthes for phenomenology, affect and mourning. "Punctum" is probably the most widely borrowed single term of the three, given how often it appears in criticism and syllabi.
- Barthes's influence on Sebald was real but partly oppositional. Writers borrow the book's form (fragments, embedded photographs, personal grief) more readily than its ontology.

### Gaps
- No sources retrieved on filmmakers (e.g. Chris Marker, Agnès Varda, Wim Wenders) explicitly engaging the book. Laura Mulvey's *Death 24x a Second* (2006) and Raymond Bellour are likely leads but were not checked.
- Hervé Guibert's *L'Image fantôme* (1981), often paired with *La Chambre claire*, was not checked. Nor were later memoirists such as Annie Ernaux, Siri Hustvedt and Maggie Nelson, or photographers who cite the book.
- Teju Cole's own published statements on Barthes (e.g. in *Known and Strange Things*, 2016) were not retrieved.
- I found no quantitative citation or syllabus-frequency data, e.g. from the Open Syllabus Project, comparing Barthes, Sontag and Benjamin.
