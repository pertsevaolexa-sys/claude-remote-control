# Polygood — LOOK CLOSER: A Material Journey

The representatives' sheet. Every stop below is a numbered marker in the 3-D

This installation guides visitors from discovering Polygood to understanding how they could use it in a project. Tiles are the central focus; the Growth Collection introduces Gensler’s contribution as product design consultant; the Oyster installation connects the two by showing how a collection material becomes an engraved architectural surface.

The journey moves left to right: the banner, brochures, Growth Collection,
LOOK CLOSER installation, engraved samples, the Translucent Collection, and
finally the conversation area.

## 4. LOOK CLOSER — the main tile demonstration

*Model marker **4**. Points at: `look-closer-installation`.*

The central installation presents a vertical 450 × 450 mm Oyster panel. Oyster belongs to the Growth Collection, so visitors now see a different application of a material they have just met.

A four-by-four grid is engraved into it. These divisions are grooves within ONE continuous sheet — not separate tiles with grout. The display shows how a material pattern and a machined surface work together.

The LOOK CLOSER sign sits at the front-left of the base, putting the invitation beside the handling sample. At the front-right is the removable, plain, unengraved Oyster sample, in portrait.

The large-panel approach reduces the number of separate pieces to install and avoids grout within the machined pattern. The 450 mm piece is a demonstration: the published Oyster production panel is 2800 × 1400 mm.

**The representative says:** “Pick up the sample and feel the material. Now look at how the same material changes when a pattern is machined into its surface.”

**Careful:** Do not promise that Polygood outlasts ceramic — there is no comparative evidence here. The supported story is the large-format machined panel and reduced grout-related upkeep.

## Conflicts to resolve before anything is made

### Main Oyster panel thickness: 12 mm vs 19 mm

The assembly drawing and the 12.1 mm base slot indicate a 12 mm panel. The cut list labels the same 450 x 450 panel as 19 mm material. A 19 mm panel cannot enter a 12.1 mm slot. The model uses 12 mm for the visual assembly. No manufacturing file has been changed.

**Decision in the model:** Modelled at 12 mm; referred for fabrication review.

### Removable coupon: drawn landscape 180 x 120, required portrait 120 x 180

The PDF slot is 190 x 15.64 x 12 mm, 60 mm from the front and offset 90 mm right, sized for a landscape coupon. The latest brief requires a portrait 120 x 180 coupon. The portrait coupon is centred in the existing 190 mm slot, leaving 35 mm of open slot at each end.

**Decision in the model:** Portrait coupon centred in the drawn slot; slot redesign referred for fabrication review, NOT approved here.

### Installation footprint vs the provisional counter depth

The installation base is 450 x 420 mm. A 20 mm margin all round needs 490 x 460 mm of flat top. The provisional counter is 450 mm deep and curved, which leaves about 26 mm of total front-to-back slack once the curve is accounted for. The object has NOT been scaled down and the counter has NOT been widened.

**Decision in the model:** Placed with about 13 mm margin front and back. Counter depth must be measured.

### Banner height: 1122.68 mm stated, about 1770 mm in the reference render

The brief gives 457.2 x 1122.68 mm (18 x 44.2 in) and warns against a two-metre banner. The supplied reference render shows a floor-standing roll-up about 1770 mm tall. Scaling its width by the same method gives 453 mm against the specified 457.2 mm, so the method is sound and the height really does disagree.

**Decision in the model:** Modelled as a floor-standing roll-up 1800 mm tall, as the reference shows, with the SPECIFIED 457.2 x 1122.68 mm graphic printed across the top and the panel blank below it. The graphic is never rescaled. One number, banner.overallHeightMm, returns it to the stated envelope.

### The counter is not long enough for both the full display run and a separate conversation area

Five display groups need about 3.1 m of the counter once sensible gaps are allowed. Three stools at the right need roughly another 1.3 m of frontage. The counter measures about 3.3 m, so the two cannot both have their own stretch. In the venue photograph the stools in fact sit IN FRONT of the display zone rather than beyond it.

**Decision in the model:** Stools kept grouped at the right as the brief requires, which puts the outer one just past the rounded end and the inner one in front of the Translucent group. Either lengthen the counter, drop to two stools, or accept the photograph's arrangement - that is a decision for the team, not a modelling choice.

### Translucent slot rotation datum is ambiguous

The 20 deg clockwise plan rotation is specified without a datum. Measured from the block width axis, ten 15 mm slots at 25 mm pitch would overlap by 6.45 mm and merge into one channel. Measured from the block depth axis they clear by 23.5 mm.

**Decision in the model:** The buildable reading (angle from the depth axis) is modelled. Confirm against the drawing.

### Counter top thickness: 45 mm legacy value vs a visibly thin top

The legacy model carries a 45 mm top. The venue photograph shows a markedly thinner top. 20 mm is used to preserve the real appearance.

**Decision in the model:** Modelled at 20 mm, provisional. Needs measuring.

### An 80 mm high block cannot be one part from 19 mm sheet

The translucent block is specified as 300 x 80 x 80 mm with 30 mm deep slots. It is represented visually as specified, but a laminated or constructed body has to be resolved before it can be made.

**Decision in the model:** Modelled as a single visual volume. Fabrication method unresolved; it does not hold up the visual model.
