Where the 8% comes from

It is measured, not chosen. In the key visual (§1.4) the chilli occupies a few per cent of a frame that is otherwise black and grey, and that single restraint is what makes the red read as heat rather than decoration. Selective colour is the colour system. Everything below follows from it.

Hard rules

One ground per layout. Ink and Navy do not appear as two grounds in the same frame or spread. Ink is film and identity; Navy is documents and commerce.

Red earns its place. Pepper Red is a rule, an underline, a live dot, one word, or the fruit. It is never a large field and never a gradient.

Green is not a UI colour. Stem Green exists inside the vector mark and as a chart or diagram colour. It is never a button, never a link, never a success state in brand-facing work. Note that the key visual has no green in it at all — if the serif route in §4.7 wins, green may leave the palette entirely.

No gradients, ever. Flat fields only. Depth comes from the frame device in §5.4, not from shading.

Consequence 1 — red is not a text colour on dark

Pepper Red on Ink reaches 4.34:1 and on Navy only 3.41:1. Both fail AA for body copy. On dark grounds red is a rule, a dot or display type at 24 px and above. Body copy on dark is Paper or Cream.

Consequence 2 — green never carries text

Stem Green misses AA on white by 0.03. Treat it as unusable for text at any size below 24 px, in any direction. It lives inside the mark, in diagrams, and nowhere else.

3.5   Colour in moving image

Broadcast & delivery

Pepper Red at full saturation sits close to the chroma ceiling for broadcast delivery. For any spot going to TV or an EU broadcaster, check it on a vectorscope and pull saturation back rather than letting the grade clip it. Never run it as a full-frame field in motion — it buzzes on compression and on LED walls.

Ink #000000 is the identity black, but for graded footage deliver to the black point the grade calls for, not to absolute zero. The slate is the only place true 0 black is required.

Navy as a gradeable ground

#04203A is deliberately dark enough to read as near-black on a phone and blue enough to separate from Ink when they sit on facing pages. Use Navy for anything commercial — decks, estimates, rate cards, the “PRICE” slate — and keep Ink for anything about the work itself.

That split is the single most useful convention in this palette: a client can tell a money document from a creative document at a glance, across the room.

Roger Excoffon, 1962. A humanist sans with an unusually large x-height, low stroke contrast and flared, slightly swollen terminals — it reads as warm and physical rather than corporate, which is exactly the brief for a production house that wants to look capable without looking cold. The supplied file is the ElseWare digitisation, v1.3.

It carries Cyrillic. 94 Cyrillic codepoints including Є, І, Ї and Ґ, so Ukrainian headlines set in the real brand face — no substitute, no fallback. This is the single most important technical fact in this section and it makes the bilingual system possible.

One weight only

There is no Regular, no Light, no Italic in this file. Never let software fake them — synthetic bold doubles the stems and synthetic italic shears the flared terminals into mush. All hierarchy in the display face comes from size, and nothing else. Set font-synthesis: none in every web build.

Two glyphs are missing

The euro € and the hryvnia ₴ are both absent from the supplied file. A production house quoting in EUR and UAH will hit this on the first rate card. Set all currency in the companion face — or commission the two glyphs. Do not substitute EUR/UAH in display type and call it a style choice.

4.2   Text face — the companion

4.6   The size-contrast device

The wordmark's big / small / big rhythm is promoted to a headline device. One word in a headline drops to 56% and the phrase closes up with no word spaces — the same construction as the logo. Use it once per layout, on the word that carries the meaning. This is the brand's typographic fingerprint.

ТРИЗМІННІДНІ

Ratio is fixed at 56%. Not 50, not 60 — 56 is what the wordmark uses, and reusing the exact figure is what makes the device read as brand behaviour rather than as a random typographic flourish.

4.7   The serif — and the conflict

Pepper Production House
PEPPERPRODUCTIONHOUSE

The same company name, twice, from the same asset folder. These are not two weights of one idea — they are two brands.

The key visual sets the name in a high-contrast serif, caps, letterspaced roughly 0.09 em, in a warm off-white. It reads classical, premium and cinematic. The lockup files set the same name in Antique Olive Bold, tight, with a heart-shaped vector pepper. That reads loud, physical and broadcast. Both are defensible. Together they are a split personality.

PropertySerif wordmark, as supplied
ClassificationHigh-contrast transitional / Didone-adjacent serif
CaseAll caps
Tracking≈ 0.09 em
ColourWarm off-white on true black
Word spacesPresent — unlike the sans lockup, which has none
Exact faceNot identified. Must be read off the source artwork.

If the serif route is chosen, standardise on a face with real Cyrillic: Cormorant Garamond is the closest match in feel and covers both scripts; PT Serif is the sturdier workhorse if the wordmark has to survive small sizes. Do not approximate it with Times New Roman.

Option A — serif leads Recommended

Serif wordmark is the name, on the key visual, the reel, the end slate, the site header. Antique Olive Bold becomes the display voice — titles, lower thirds, section slates, call sheets. The vector pepper is retired or demoted to an app icon.

Why: it is more distinctive, it matches twenty years of broadcast pedigree, and it gives the system two clearly separated jobs — serif says the name, sans does the talking. It also keeps the strongest asset you have at the centre.

Option B — sans leads

Antique Olive lockup stays primary everywhere. The key visual is reissued with the sans wordmark and the serif is dropped.

Why not: it costs you the best thing in the set. The chunky sans on a baroque sculpture fights the photograph rather than sitting on it.

Option C — two tiers

Serif as a "house" presentation mark, sans as the working masterbrand, each with defined territory.

Workable but expensive: two marks means two sets of files, two rules and a decision to make every time anyone opens a document. Only choose this deliberately.

Do not ship until this is decided

Everything downstream — the file set, the deck template, the title package, the site header — depends on which wordmark is the wordmark. This document currently specifies the sans lockup in §2 because that is what arrived as finished logo files, and flags the serif here. That is a holding position, not an answer.

7.2   The rules of movement

Type moves one way

Type enters by cut or wipe, never by fade, and never scales or rotates. A wipe reveals left-to-right in Latin, left-to-right in Cyrillic. The red rule draws first, the type follows.

The mark has an ident, not a wiggle

This house is led by the art director who built M1 and M2's on-air identity, so a proper ident is core equity, not an indulgence — see §7.4. What is banned is decoration: no bouncing pepper, no growing stem, no squash-and-stretch, and no animation on the wordmark itself. Outside the ident, the mark cuts on and cuts off.

Easing

One curve for UI: cubic-bezier(.2,.7,.2,1), 220 ms. One for picture: a hard linear on wipes. Nothing in this brand overshoots or springs.

Reduced motion

Every web transition sits behind prefers-reduced-motion. When it is set, states change instantly and the load-in is skipped entirely — the layout must be complete and legible with all animation removed.

One sting, in one place. The ident may announce the house at the head; the end slate stays quiet. Houses that sting themselves twice are selling the house — we are selling the film.

7.4   The ident

A house that sells broadcast design needs an ident that proves it. The idea is already written by the key visual, and it needs no character animation: hold the classical group in cold greyscale, and let the red arrive. The pepper is the only thing that moves or changes.

BeatFrames @ 25fpsWhat happens
1 · Hold0–12Full greyscale frame, static. No red anywhere.
2 · Arrival12–20The pepper saturates to Pepper Red over 8 frames. Nothing else changes. Sting lands on frame 12.
3 · Hold20–45Static, red present.
4 · Wordmark45–53Wordmark wipes on beneath, left to right.
5 · Out53–60Hard cut. No fade, no zoom-out.

Total 60 frames — 2.4 seconds. Short enough to survive a pre-roll, long enough to register. A 30-frame cutdown drops beats 1 and 3.

Rules

The camera never moves. Locked off. The drama is entirely in the colour arriving.

Only the pepper changes. No relighting, no particles, no lens flare, no rack focus.

One red, once. The saturation ramps up and stays. It does not pulse, breathe or flicker.

Build it once. Then it is a file, not a job. Per-project ident variants are how houses lose an ident.

Why this and not a logo animation

Animating a flat vector pepper makes the brand look like a food app. Letting colour arrive in a baroque marble frame looks like a production house that knows what it is doing — and it is executable in-house with the real-time stack in §1.3.

§ 8

Image language

Two registers: the composed world the brand presents itself in, and the working set it actually lives on. Мова зображення

Client work looks like the client. This section governs everything where Pepper is the subject. It has two registers and they must not be blended: the canon, which is the brand's own composed imagery, and the set, which is the honest documentary of work happening.

8.1   Register one — the canon

The reference frame for every canon image

Classical sculpture on true black, drained to cold greyscale, with exactly one object in Pepper Red. Used for the key visual, the ident, campaign leads, the site hero, poster treatments and the reel packaging. It is the brand's face and it is built, not found.

PropertyDirection
SubjectBaroque and neoclassical sculpture, plaster casts, marble, architectural statuary. Figures caught mid-gesture — reaching, offering, raising.
GroundTrue black, edge to edge. The subject floats. No vignette, no gradient, no seamless-paper studio look.
LightHard, raking side light that finds the carving. One key, deep falloff, no fill on the shadow side.
GradeFull desaturation of everything except the pepper. Cold neutral to slightly blue in the stone. Crushed blacks are correct here.
The redOne object, always the pepper, always genuinely red — never a tinted overlay or a colourised approximation.
CompositionCentred or classically weighted. Generous black around the subject; the frame is mostly empty.
LensLonger glass, 85–135mm, for compression and flatness. The figure should feel carved, not photographed.

The one-red rule

Never two red objects in a canon frame. The moment there are two, the device stops being a statement and becomes a colour scheme. If a second element needs emphasis, it gets light, not colour.

Extending it

The system is a classical figure and a pepper, not this one statue. A hand from a frieze offering the chilli; a bust with one on the plinth; a caryatid holding one instead of an entablature. Same black, same grade, same single red. Shoot them properly — real casts and museum statuary, or build them in the real-time stack. Never AI-composite a pepper into stock.

Never

Teal-and-orange. Halation and bloom. Colourised or duotoned stone. A red-tinted background. The pepper lying on a surface — it is always held, offered or raised, because that gesture is the whole idea. Cherub imagery used cutely: this is baroque sculpture, not Valentine's.

8.2   Register two — the set

Everything that proves the work is real: behind the scenes, the volume, the gallery, crew portraits, studio and technology. This register is the opposite of the canon — warm, honest, unstaged — and that contrast is deliberate. The canon says what we are; the set shows what we do.

11.3   The short brief

If only one paragraph survives the handoff, this is it:

Pepper Production House is a Kyiv media production house built on twenty years of broadcast — formats, shows and national awards, branded film for global clients, and an in-house real-time stack (AR, real-time 3D, virtual production, AI). Its CEO, Sergiy Pertsev, was CEO, general producer and art director at the M1 and M2 channels and personally made their on-air identity. The brand's central image is a baroque marble group in cold greyscale with exactly one object in colour: a red chilli, held aloft. That single restraint is the colour system — Ink #000000 for the work, Navy #04203A for the money, Cream #FCE2C9 as the frame, Pepper Red #E01B16 at under a tenth of any layout. Display type is Antique Olive Bold, uppercase, one weight, 0.94 leading; reading type is a humanist sans with real Cyrillic; the key visual sets the name in a high-contrast serif instead. Its signature typographic move is the wordmark's big/small/big rhythm at exactly 56%, used once per layout. Corners are soft on cards (28 px) and square on picture (0). No gradients, no shadows on the logo, no animation except one 60-frame ident where the red simply arrives. Everything is bilingual UA/EN, lead language at 100%, second at 68%, split by a 3 px red rule. One thing is unresolved: the assets contain two wordmarks — a serif and a sans — and §4.7 recommends the serif leads.

12.1   Asset flags — read before using this in production

Two wordmarks — decide this first

The key visual sets the name in a high-contrast serif; the logo files set it in Antique Olive Bold with a vector pepper. These are two identities, not two versions of one. §4.7 lays out three options and recommends the serif leading with Antique Olive as the display voice. Nothing — file set, deck, titles, site — can be built until this is answered.

The black-card lockup is misspelled

On the cream-framed black card, the middle word reads PODUCTION — the r is missing. The two white-background lockups are correct. That file should not be used anywhere until it is replaced, and it is worth checking whether it has already gone out on a slate or a deck.

The pepper here is a reconstruction

The mark in this document is hand-drawn SVG matched to the supplied PNGs, so the manual renders without external files. It is close, not identical — the original outline is more irregular. Replace #pepper with the real vector from the source artwork before this becomes the reference file.

Colour values are eye-sampled

All six hex values were read off flattened PNGs, not pulled from source vectors, and the Pantone references are nearest-neighbour guesses rather than a measured bridge. Confirm both before print. §11 is the single place to correct them.

Antique Olive licensing

Antique Olive is a licensed commercial typeface. The supplied file embeds fine for internal use, but a public web build needs a webfont licence, and a client deliverable may need the type outlined. Confirm the licence terms before shipping the font in anything public.

One weight is a real constraint

Bold only, no italic. The system is designed around it, but if the studio expects to set long-form treatments in the brand face, buying or commissioning a lighter weight is the single highest-value typographic investment available.

Still placeholder

Sergiy Pertsev's name and role, the project list and the client list are now real, from the supplied CEO document. Still invented: the domain, the phone number, and the crew names in the §6.3 credit block. Replace the credit names before that block is used anywhere — it should only ever carry people who actually worked on the job.

Errors in the CEO bio PDF

The supplied bio has the Russian word внедрение sitting in the English paragraph where implementing belongs, and кінопродашкну for кінопродакшну in the Ukrainian. Both are corrected in §1.5 here, but the source document needs fixing — it is presumably going out to clients in that state.

The serif face is unidentified

The wordmark serif on the key visual is baked into a JPEG, so the actual typeface is unknown. §4.7 recommends Cormorant Garamond as the closest Cyrillic-capable match, but the real face should be read off the source artwork before anything is set in it.
