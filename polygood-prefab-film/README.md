# Polygood prefabrication film

A 30-second motion piece on Polygood's in-house prefabrication, from a technical drawing to finished parts, ending on the Polygood logo animation. Built with Remotion, React and TypeScript. Two compositions share every scene and differ only in layout:

| Composition | Size | Ratio | Length |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PolygoodPrefab-Reels` | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 | 900 frames at 30 fps (30 s) |
| `PolygoodPrefab-LinkedIn` | 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 | 900 frames at 30 fps (30 s) |
| `PolygoodRealistic-Reels` | 1080 × 1920 | 9:16 | 900 frames at 30 fps (30 s), photoreal 3D |
| `PolygoodRealistic-LinkedIn` | 1080 × 1350 | 4:5 | 900 frames at 30 fps (30 s), photoreal 3D |

The original brief asked for 15 s. The film was later lengthened to 30 s so every headline and label can be read, and the end card was replaced by the supplied logo animation, played as is.

Rendered films (2D and realistic) and storyboard sheets are in [`renders/`](renders/).

> **Placeholders.** No material scan, renders, reference video or audio were supplied (only the fonts, the logo and the logo animation). The material is a procedural terrazzo-style texture and the finish identifier reads `PS 000 · Placeholder finish`. Both are clearly marked and are swapped in one config file (see [Replacing assets](#replacing-assets)). Don't publish the film until they are replaced.

## Quick start

```console
npm i
npm run dev              # Remotion Studio: preview, scrub, edit
npm run render           # both films to out/
npm run render:reels     # out/polygood-prefab-reels-1080x1920.mp4
npm run render:linkedin  # out/polygood-prefab-linkedin-1080x1350.mp4
npm run lint             # eslint + typecheck
```

If Remotion can't download its headless browser (offline or sandboxed machines), point it at a local Chromium:

```console
REMOTION_BROWSER_EXECUTABLE=/path/to/chrome-headless-shell npm run render
```

To check frames without rendering the whole film: `node scripts/stills.mjs PolygoodPrefab-Reels out/stills 0 300 899 --contact`.

## Realistic version (Blender)

`PolygoodRealistic-*` tell the same story with scenes 01–05 rendered in Blender (Cycles) instead of drawn in 2D. They keep the same headlines, spec lines, checklist, sound and logo clip, but carry no finish tag. Their soundtrack is mastered louder for phones: −14 LUFS instead of −23 (see [Audio](#audio)). The 3D scene is generated by Python from the same config as the 2D film, so every move lands on the same frame as its headline, label and sound cue.

What the camera sees:

- a 2800 × 1400 × 19 mm terrazzo sheet, textured from the material scan by real sheet coordinates, with the macro crops in the opening push-in;
- a pen plotting the drawing in white ink;
- a gantry CNC router cutting the nest, with kerfs trailing the bit;
- the tabletop turning to its end face, its edge morphing between profiles (shape keys);
- the slotted partition machined, then bent upright;
- pallets of full sheets and finished parts, with offcuts breaking into chips that land as a new strip.

The lighting is three soft area lights plus a dim world, rendered through the Khronos PBR Neutral view transform. The renders have transparent backgrounds and keep their shadows on a shadow catcher, so Remotion supplies the paper colour and the cut into the logo clip stays seamless.

Remotion draws everything that must stay crisp and editable: the type, the orange edge contour and the thickness callout (placed from 3D points projected by the Blender camera, `anchors.json`), the stack labels, and the toolpath wipe from scene 03 into scene 04.

To rebuild and render (needs Python 3.13 and `pip install bpy==5.2.2`, or Blender's own Python):

```console
npm run blender:export                            # TS config → blender/scene-data.json
python blender/build.py --variant reels           # → blender/blend/polygood-reels.blend, anchors.json, frames.json
python blender/build.py --variant linkedin
PYTHON=python sh blender/render-all.sh            # → public/renders3d/<variant>/frame_0000.png … frame_0749.png
npm run render:realistic                          # out/polygood-realistic-{reels-1080x1920,linkedin-1080x1350}.mp4
```

If Blender's colour config isn't found (the pip `bpy` on some systems), set `OCIO=<site-packages>/bpy/5.2/datafiles/colormanagement/config.ocio`.

Rendering:

- **Settings:** Cycles on CPU at 16 samples with OpenImageDenoise.
- **Speed:** about 25 s per frame on 4 cores, or roughly 4.5 h for Reels and 3 h for LinkedIn.
- **Holds:** identical frames (see `frames.json`) render once and are copied.
- **Resumable:** the script skips frames that already exist.
- **Not committed:** the PNG sequences (about 2 GB) are gitignored. The `.blend` files are committed, so the scene can be opened, inspected or re-lit in Blender 5.x.

Change the scene in `blender/pgscene/` and rebuild, rather than editing the `.blend` by hand: `build.py` also regenerates the anchors the overlays are placed from.

Two things the composite does instead of the 3D:

- **End of scene 05:** the film holds the last 3D frame before the stacks clear and dissolves it to paper. Partly transparent slabs in Cycles show their insides as they fade.
- **Scene 04 headline and its LinkedIn spec lines:** set on paper labels, as in scene 01, because the partition rises behind them as it stands up.

## Storyboard

| # | Time | Frames | On screen | What happens |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | 0–4 s | 0–119 | Start with your drawing | Extreme macro of the material, light sweep, pull-back to the full sheet. A pen plots a plan and end elevations of a table and bench, then dimension lines and ticks (no numbers). |
| 02 | 4–9 s | 120–269 | Prefabricated in-house | The drawing separates into six parts that slide into a nested layout on a 2800 × 1400 mm sheet. An orange CAM toolpath cuts each part (dashed rapids between them), the parts lift off the dimmed skeleton, and the tabletop moves to centre. The checklist appears as the scene starts; **Cut** ticks. |
| 03 | 9–14 s | 270–419 | Your edge, your profile | The tabletop turns and tips (CSS 3D) until only its end face shows, which becomes a 2D cross-section. The camera pushes in on the edge, which morphs square → bullnose → chamfer → bevelled, with the active label and an orange contour. Thickness callout. **Edged** ticks. |
| 04 | 14–19.5 s | 420–584 | Curved without thermoforming | A toolpath crosses the frame, wiping the edge close-up away to reveal a second panel. The toolpath engraves a staggered pattern of capsule cut-outs; the plugs drop away; the panel bends into a curved partition built from 40 strips in 3D, with a floor shadow tracing the curve. **Machined** and **Curved** tick. |
| 05 | 19.5–25 s | 585–749 | Lighter to ship, less waste | Isometric delivery stacks: full sheets beside the shorter stack of finished parts (dry tap as they settle). Offcuts move away, break into chips and re-form as a strip of new panel material, which then clears. |
| 06 | 25–30 s | 750–899 | (logo) | The supplied logo animation, played as is: the mark draws and fills, then "polygood" and "by the good plastic company". Static for the final second; silent for the final 0.5 s. |

The finish identifier sits bottom-left until just before the logo animation. LinkedIn adds the technical spec lines from the brief beneath each headline.

## Design system

- **Type:** PP Neue Montreal (Book for headlines and labels, Bold for the PS code), black on off-white `#F4F3ED` (matched to the logo animation's background, so the cut into it is invisible). Sentence case, five words or fewer per headline, set line by line behind masks. If the font files are missing, `config/typography.ts` logs a warning and falls back to Inter / Helvetica Neue instead of failing the render.
- **Colour:** the material keeps its own colours. Orange `#FF5A1F` marks anything the machine does (toolpaths, tool head, active profile, checkmarks). The drawing is plotted in white on the material and dimensioned in black on paper.
- **Grid:** 80 px side margins on Reels (72 on LinkedIn). On Reels, key content stays between y = 250 and y = 1500 to clear the platform UI.
- **Motion:** calm rather than snappy. Eases in `config/theme.ts` keep peak speed low (ease-in-out for camera moves, ease-out cubic for arrivals), and headline lines take 22 frames to rise and 13 to leave. Hero objects fill most of the stage. Every value is computed from the frame (`interpolate`, eased with Béziers). There are no CSS transitions and no randomness beyond seeded `random()`, so any frame renders the same way every time.

Geometry is in millimetres on the real sheet. The nest is 1600 × 800 tabletop, 1600 × 360 bench seat and four arched slab legs, with 30 mm or more between parts. Scenes look at it through a camera, so lines stay sharp at any zoom.

## Replacing assets

Everything is referenced from `src/config/assets.ts`. Change it there, not in the scenes.

1. **Material.** Replace `public/images/placeholder-material-sheet.jpg` with a scan of a full 2800 × 1400 mm sheet (2:1, about 4096 px wide), and update `ASSETS.material.sheet`. The opening macro uses two higher-resolution crops listed in `MATERIAL_LEVELS`. Export matching crops of the same scan at those regions, or empty the array (the macro will be softer). The swatch in the finish tag, the parts, the panel and the recycled strip all crop the same image, so they stay consistent. The Blender scene reads the same files: rebuild and re-render the realistic version after replacing them.
2. **Finish identifier.** Set `FINISH.code` and `FINISH.pattern` to the finish shown, then set `isPlaceholder: false`.
3. **Thickness.** `CLAIMS.thicknessLabel` reads "12 or 19 mm" because the real component's thickness isn't known. Set it to "19 mm" or "12 mm" once it is.
4. **Logo animation.** `public/video/polygood-linedraw.mp4` is the supplied clip (1920 × 1080, 150 frames, no audio), described in `ASSETS.endCardVideo`. The end card scales it so the lockup is `endCard.lockupWidthPx` wide (`config/layout.ts`) and centres it; the clip itself is not edited. If you replace it, update `durationInFrames`, `background` and `contentBox` (where the lockup sits in the clip). The static logo SVGs supplied earlier are kept in `public/images/` but aren't used.
5. **Fonts.** These are `public/fonts/PPNeueMontreal-{Book,Bold}.otf`. Check that the Neue Montreal licence covers video use for this campaign.

To regenerate the placeholders: `npm run assets:material` and `npm run assets:audio`.

## Audio

All sound is synthesised by `scripts/generate_audio.py` (numpy only): pad, pen-plotter scratch, spindle whir, checklist clicks, three mechanical clicks for the profiles, CNC engraving, panel flex, a dry tap, and one resolving note as the logo's wordmark completes. The logo clip has no audio of its own. Being generated from scratch, none of it carries a third-party licence, so it is safe to publish. The sounds are deliberately restrained placeholders. Each cue is its own file in `public/audio/` and is placed in `src/config/audio.ts`, so any one can be swapped for a licensed library sound.

- Cue times are derived from the scene beats in `timing.ts`, so they move with the picture.
- Loading the project fails if any cue runs into the final 0.5 s (frame 885 onwards).
- Rendered mix: −23.3 LUFS integrated, −8.3 dBFS true peak, digital silence from 29.5 s.
- The realistic films' render scripts then run `scripts/master_audio.py`, which raises the mix to −14 LUFS (the usual level for Instagram and LinkedIn) behind a −2 dBFS limiter. At −23 LUFS the soundtrack is barely audible on a phone. Run it on the 2D films too if they are posted as they are.

**Voice-over (optional, not recorded).** Script: *Start with your drawing. We prefabricate in-house: cut to shape, edged in the profile you need, CNC milled, engraved, or cut to bend without thermoforming. Lighter to ship. Offcuts become new panels. Send your drawing.* Record it confident and understated, keep it inside 29.5 s, save it as `public/audio/voiceover.wav` and set `VOICEOVER` in `config/audio.ts`. Lower the pad volume when it's in.

## Validation

`src/config/validate.ts` checks on every load (Studio and render) that:

- the total is at most 30 s and the six scenes are contiguous;
- the checklist appears with scene 02, the finish identifier is gone before the logo animation, and the end card plays the whole clip with its final second static;
- the last 0.5 s is silent;
- the formats are 1080 × 1920 and 1080 × 1350.

On the rendered files I confirmed the following:

- Both are H.264 + AAC, 900 frames at 30 fps, and 30.0 s long.
- Frames 870 and 899 are visually identical (PSNR 53–55 dB). What difference there is comes from a faint easing tail in the supplied clip itself.
- The audio from 29.5 s is at −91 dB.
- The realistic films check out the same way: 900 frames, 30.0 s, −14.0 LUFS (true peak −1.9 dBFS), −91 dB from 29.5 s, and frames 870 and 899 at a PSNR of 55 dB (Reels) and 53.5 dB (LinkedIn).
- Scene boundaries are continuous: the S2→S3 tabletop handover lines up, S4 opens on S3's last frame and wipes it away, and S5 clears to paper before the logo clip, which opens on the same paper colour.

## Missing assets and claims to confirm

**Not supplied** (drawn procedurally or left as integration points): reference video, material scans, tabletop-and-bench line drawing, component renders, curved-partition render, CNC toolpath artwork, audio, voice-over.

**To confirm before publishing:**

- The real finish and its PS code and pattern name, and a matching scan.
- The tabletop thickness (12 or 19 mm).
- That production offcuts are recycled into new panels. The scene 05 visual and its "Offcuts become new panels" label follow the voice-over script, and the visual is abstract on purpose. It doesn't suggest a customer take-back programme.
- The film no longer carries a call to action or URL (the end card is the logo animation only, as requested). Add them back if the post copy doesn't cover them.
- That the cut-out partition pattern matches a technique Polygood offers. It is drawn as through-cut openings, not kerf cuts, with no heat shown.
- The spec lines (panel size, thicknesses, edge profiles, CNC options), which are used exactly as given in the brief.
- The table and bench are generic illustrations, not a client project.
